ABSTRACT
================================================================================
• Paragraph 1: Context & Need
  - Diagnostic error rates in emergency and primary care (cognitive fatigue, 
    time constraints, subtle symptom interactions).
  - Vulnerability of black-box AI and ungrounded LLMs (hallucinations, 
    uncalibrated probabilities).
• Paragraph 2: System Overview
  - Introduction of MedAgentix: A multi-agent clinical decision support framework.
  - Architecture: Decomposition into specialized agents for intake, triage, 
    symptom extraction, differential diagnosis, calibration, XAI, and RAG.
• Paragraph 3: Data & Methods
  - Multi-source data: Aggregation of public biomedical benchmark repositories 
    merged with retrospective observational hospital clinical records.
  - Methods: Ensemble tree learning (XGBoost, RF, LightGBM), Platt calibration, 
    TreeSHAP + LIME explainability, and PubMedBERT semantic retrieval.
• Paragraph 4: Quantitative Results & Clinical Impact
  - 82.98% Top-1 accuracy and 96.53% Top-5 accuracy across 721 diseases.
  - Zero-under-triage (0.0% UTR) and 100% critical sensitivity on emergency cases.
  - End-to-end pipeline latency under 350 ms, validating clinical utility.

================================================================================
1. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================
1.1 Clinical Background & Motivation
  - High volume of patients presenting to emergency departments and outpatient 
    clinics with non-specific, overlapping symptom profiles.
  - The cognitive burden on attending physicians: simultaneous synthesis of 
    free-text symptoms, historical vitals, comorbidity flags, and lab ranges.
1.2 Problem Statement
  - Traditional rule-based CDSS are rigid and fail to adapt to complex cases.
  - Deep learning models act as uninterpretable black boxes, creating clinical 
    skepticism.
  - LLMs hallucinate plausible yet erroneous diagnoses and lack deterministic 
    safety constraints.
1.3 Research Objectives
  - Formulate an autonomous multi-agent clinical reasoning system.
  - Ensure algorithmic safety with zero under-triage of emergency presentations.
  - Deliver dual-format explainability (mathematical charts for doctors, 
    plain-language summaries for patients).
  - Ground clinical QA in validated medical literature via dense retrieval.
1.4 Key Contributions (List as 4-5 bullet points)
  - Modular Multi-Agent Architecture with supervisor dispute arbitration.
  - High-coverage differential diagnosis engine covering 721 disease categories.
  - Calibrated confidence reporting using Platt scaling.
  - Safety-first triage protocol with 0.0% under-triage rate.
  - Empirical validation on both benchmark datasets and hospital cohorts.
1.5 Organization of the Paper
  - Brief roadmap paragraph describing Sections 2 through 7.

================================================================================
2. LITERATURE SURVEY
================================================================================
2.1 Clinical Decision Support Systems (CDSS)
  - Historical trajectory: Rule-based engines (MYCIN, DXplain) to supervised ML.
2.2 Machine Learning in Medical Diagnosis
  - Effectiveness of Gradient-Boosted Trees (XGBoost, LightGBM) on tabular 
    clinical records compared to deep neural networks.
2.3 Multi-Agent Systems in Healthcare
  - How agent specialization, task decomposition, and debate improve diagnostic 
    robustness over monolithic single-model systems.
2.4 Explainable AI (XAI) in Medicine
  - Comparison of SHAP (game-theoretic Shapley values) and LIME (local surrogates).
  - Importance of tailoring explanations to clinical vs. patient audiences.
2.5 Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in Healthcare
  - Using biomedical language models (PubMedBERT) for dense semantic retrieval 
    to eliminate LLM hallucinations.
2.6 Summary Table of Related Work
  - Table comparing existing systems (IBM Watson, Google Streams, Ada, Med-PaLM) 
    against MedAgentix on Agent Collaboration, Safety Audit, Calibrated Probabilities, 
    and Dual XAI.

================================================================================
3. DATA COLLECTION & PREPROCESSING
================================================================================
3.1 Multi-Source Clinical Data Acquisition
  - Public Clinical Benchmark Repositories: Aggregated from peer-reviewed public 
    clinical registries and disease-symptom benchmark corpora on Kaggle and 
    biomedical repositories (encompassing multi-disease symptom profiles, 
    risk factors, and physiological records).
  - Hospital Observational Cohort: Retrospective de-identified clinical records 
    collected from partner hospital inpatient and outpatient encounters for real-world 
    validation and clinician concordance auditing.
3.2 Ethical Considerations & Patient Anonymization
  - Adherence to HIPAA Safe Harbor guidelines; all 18 direct patient identifiers 
    removed prior to model training and evaluation.
  - Institutional governance compliance for retrospective observational data.
3.3 Feature Harmonization & Standardization
  - Ontological mapping of colloquial patient terms to standardized medical 
    nomenclatures (SNOMED-CT, ICD-10).
  - Unified feature schema combining continuous vitals, binary symptom presence, 
    demographics, and temporal duration buckets.
3.4 Missing Value Imputation & Normalization
  - Median imputation for missing vital signs stratified by demographic bands.
  - Standardized scaling (z-score normalization) for continuous physiological signals.
3.5 Train-Test Splitting
  - Patient-stratified 70/15/15 train, validation, and test split, preserving 
    label distribution across all 721 disease categories.

4. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY: MULTI-AGENT ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================
4.1 Decentralized Multi-Agent Philosophy
  - Explain why specialized agents outperform a single monolithic model: 
    modular auditing, safety isolation, and domain-specific optimization.
4.2 Functional Clinical Agent Roles
  - Intake & Triage Agent: Emergency Severity Index (ESI) mapping and vital red-flag triggers.
  - Symptom Extraction Agent: Bio_ClinicalBERT for token-level symptom and severity extraction.
  - Differential Diagnosis Engine: Multi-class XGBoost + LightGBM + Random Forest ensemble.
  - Risk Assessment Agent: Comorbidity scoring (cardiovascular, diabetic, lifestyle factors).
  - Temporal Agent: Acute (<7 days), subacute, and chronic progression analysis.
  - Recommendation Agent: Evidence-based drug formulary and diagnostic test mapping.
  - Supervisor Agent: Confidence-based routing, debate arbitration, and clinician escalation.
4.3 Probability Calibration via Platt Scaling
  - Converting raw tree margins into true empirical posterior probabilities using 
    logistic sigmoid transformation.
4.4 Dual-Method Explainable AI (SHAP + LIME)
  - TreeSHAP for exact feature attribution; LIME for local stability verification.
  - Dual presentation: Technical waterfall charts for doctors; plain language for patients.
4.5 Grounded Medical Knowledge Retrieval (RAG)
  - Dense indexing with PubMedBERT vector embeddings; retrieval over WHO, CDC, 
    and clinical guidelines to eliminate generative hallucinations.
