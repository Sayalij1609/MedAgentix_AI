1. EVALUATION METHODOLOGY OVERVIEW
================================================================================

The MedAgentix AI evaluation framework is a four-phase benchmark pipeline designed
to rigorously assess the performance, reliability, and clinical safety of all 10
specialized medical AI agents that form the system's multi-agent architecture.

Phase 1 — Test Data Generation:
    223 curated test cases were generated across 7 categories (triage, temporal,
    emergency, risk, symptom extraction, recommendation, and end-to-end pipeline).
    These cases span a spectrum of clinical severity from routine low-acuity
    presentations to life-threatening emergencies.

Phase 2 — Per-Agent Evaluation:
    Each agent is instantiated independently and tested against its domain-specific
    test dataset. Metrics are computed for accuracy, recall, precision, coverage,
    latency, and domain-specific clinical quality indicators.

Phase 3 — Clinical Safety Audit:
    A cross-agent safety audit verifies that the system never under-triages
    critical patients (i.e., no patient with a life-threatening condition is
    classified as low-priority). This is the most important metric in the entire
    benchmark — a single failure here would disqualify the system.

Phase 4 — Report Generation:
    Machine-readable JSON and human-readable Markdown reports are generated,
    along with this interpretive text document.

The Differential Diagnosis Agent is the system's core diagnostic engine. It takes

multi-class classification problem.

Top-1 Accuracy at 82.98% means that the correct disease is the model's first

More importantly, Top-3 Accuracy at 94.01% and Top-5 Accuracy at 96.53% mean

The gap between Macro F1 (66.12%) and Weighted F1 (82.65%) reveals a class

(with fewer training samples) have lower per-class F1 scores. Macro F1 weights
all 721 diseases equally regardless of frequency, so rare conditions drag the
average down. Weighted F1, which accounts for class frequency, shows the model
performs well on the conditions it encounters most.

All 5 live validation cases (end-to-end symptom-to-diagnosis calls) succeeded,
confirming the agent works correctly in production-like conditions.

The 150.47 ms latency is acceptable for clinical decision support, though it is
the slowest agent due to the scale of the XGBoost model.

predictions. This is critical for clinical AI systems where physicians need to
understand why a particular diagnosis was suggested.

Note: Faithfulness evaluation (whether the SHAP explanations actually correspond
to the model's true reasoning) requires manual clinician review and cannot be

6. LIMITATIONS AND CAVEATS
================================================================================

1. TEST SET SIZES:
   - Risk Assessment (8 cases), Supervisor routing (4 cases), and Symptom
     normalization (8 cases) have small test sets. While results are encouraging,
     statistical confidence intervals are wide at these sample sizes.
   - The Differential Diagnosis Agent has the strongest statistical backing
     with 49,365 test samples.

2. SYNTHETIC TEST DATA:
   - Test cases were generated from curated clinical scenarios rather than
     real patient records. Real-world clinical data contains noise, ambiguity,
     and edge cases that synthetic data may not fully capture.

3. OCR EVALUATION GAP:
   - The OCR Agent was only tested for initialization and knowledge base
     coverage. Full accuracy evaluation requires real medical document images
     with ground truth annotations.

4. LLM FALLBACK NOT TESTED:
   - The Supervisor Agent's Groq LLaMA-3.3-70B fallback path was not
     quantitatively evaluated. LLM responses are inherently variable and
     require separate evaluation protocols.

5. SHAP FAITHFULNESS:
   - The XAI Engine produces explanations, but whether those explanations
     faithfully represent the model's true decision process requires manual
     clinician review — automated faithfulness metrics are not yet integrated.

6. CLASS IMBALANCE IN DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS:
   - The gap between Macro F1 (66.12%) and Weighted F1 (82.65%) indicates that
     rare diseases are predicted less reliably than common ones. Patients with
     rare conditions may receive less accurate initial differentials.
